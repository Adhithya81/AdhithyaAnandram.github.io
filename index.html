Projects.

Gradient Light Video Projection-Based DLP
Video projection-based stereolithography (VP-SL) enables high-resolution printing but suffers from voids, poor surface finish, and size limitations. Gradient Light VP-SL (GLVP-SL) uses grayscale masks and continuous resin flow to overcome these challenges.

Objectives: Develop and validate a novel Gradient Light Video Projection-based Stereolithography (GLVP-SL) process with continuous resin flow that can produce void-free, solid 3D objects with good surface quality by optimizing grayscale mask distributions.

Results: Using three grayscale masks per layer decreased voids and reduced surface roughness compared to VP-SL. The optimized grayscale distribution ensured consistent curing and defect-free solids across different geometries.
Single Layer Delamination Simulation (Ansys)
The delamination of cured layer from PDMS surface is simulated using Ansys by having hyperelastic material condition for PDMS and linear elastic for hydrogel. The contact region is set to bonded and the separation method using cohesive zone modelling.

Objectives: Simulate delamination process of cured layer from PDMS surface using Neo-Hookean model.

Results: Converged solution represents the complete delamination of cured layer of hydrogel from PDMS with different plots depicting normal and von-mises stresses matching the experimental data.
Top-Down DLP 3D Printer Assembly
High viscous resins were hard to print using bottom-up DLP system which necessitated to build a top-down version with automatic z-stage movement and recoating mechanism. Along with it, extrusion mechanism and sintering system is integrated to automate conductive polymer printing process.

Objectives: Develop a top-down DLP system along with extrusion system and sintering gun to facilitate printing conductive polymers filled with nano copper or nano silver particles.

Results: Successfully developed and prototyped hybrid 3D printing system and validated system interaction by 3D printing objects with varying geometric complexities.
Resin Formulation for DLP 3D Printing
Photosensitive resin formulations were developed by plotting Jacob working curve that compares the effect of energy density vs cured thickness. These formulations were further refined using rheometry data to accurately identify how the viscosity of the resin changes with shear rate and alter the composition with the addition of reactive diluent. Printability aspect of these resins were assessed by printing different geometries.

Objectives: Formulate photosensitive resins that enables to achieving high detail features with shorter print time and curing energy.

Results: Printed samples were investigated under confocal optical microscope for its dimensional accuracy and the tensile strength data were collected to compare print orientation vs tensile strength.
Ultra-Low Adhesive Energy Interface
Inspired by the pitcher plant's slippery peristome, which prevents adhesion through a thin water layer, we developed a universal ultra-low adhesive curing surface for DLP 3D printing. This bioinspired interface minimizes vertical adhesion for easy part release and reduces horizontal shear for rapid resin refilling, enabling continuous printing of complex structures within minutes instead of hours.

Objectives: To reduce tension force while delaminating cured layers from PDMS surface using Bottom-up DLP printing.

Results: The micro-pores helps to retain fluorinated oil which in turn lowered the tension force while 3D printing by minimizing the contact surface between the cured layer and PDMS.
Digital Twin for Vehicle Steering & Suspension
To emulate the attachment points of the suspension components to the vehicle chassis, the pivot points on the A-Arms (upper and lower control arms), one end of the steering column, and the upper end of the spring are constrained or fixed in position. This constraint strategy not only simulates the actual mounting points but also ensures that the desired degrees of freedom are preserved, allowing translation only in the intended directions during the simulations.

Objectives: To perform static structural and modal analysis on the front suspension system of a racing car.

Results: Most of the dynamic loads are predominantly absorbed by the springs and the shock helps to mitigate reactional forces. The response curve provides a comprehensive picture of the stress induced in the suspension and the steering components. This design of experiments can be used as the computing logic behind the digital twin for real time control of the dynamics of the car. Dynamic or transient analysis would provide optimum reduced order models compared to static structural analysis.
In-Situ Flaw Detection for Electron Beam AM
Real-time porosity detection and process parameter control for E-beam metal additive process helps to minimize print failure and material wastage. A Python program trained to detect porosity using CT and BSE data is used to identify pores from NIR data, used to adjust beam power, velocity, and hatch spacing.

Objective: Predict porosity for a given layer using NIR data and validate against CT and BSE.

Results: Higher accuracy detecting pores from NIR images vs BSE, with CT validation confirming effectiveness for real-time monitoring.
